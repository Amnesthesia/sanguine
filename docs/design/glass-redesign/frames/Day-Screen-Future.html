Food and movement

Days before your period · expected

What is changing

A typical cycle, drawn to explain. Not your own hormone levels.

Progesterone and estrogen fall in the days before the period. Many women notice lower mood, bloating, cravings or lighter sleep in these days.

Calcium-rich foods

 Some

Milk, yoghurt, cheese, calcium-fortified soy drinks, tofu set with calcium or canned salmon with the bones.

Yoghurt with fruit Cheese and salad sandwich Tofu stir-fry

In a 10-year study of more than 3,000 nurses, women who got the most calcium from food were about 40% less likely to develop premenstrual symptoms, and in a trial of 466 women calcium eased them more than a placebo did.4,5 Calcium also supports bone health at every age.

Slow-digesting carbohydrate

 Early

Regular meals with oats, brown rice, wholegrain bread, sweet potato or legumes.

Sweet potato and black bean bowl Wholegrain toast with peanut butter

Why it is only early: cravings for carbohydrate are common before a period. Regular wholegrain meals are a common suggestion for steadier energy and mood, but few trials have tested it.

Movement that lifts your mood

 Strong

When your mood or energy is low, a walk, a bike ride, a swim, yoga or a dance class can lift it. A regular wind-down before bed can help with sleep.8

1,000+ trialsReviews find exercise improves mood; walking, yoga and strength training do especially well.1,2 15 trialsWomen who exercised reported fewer premenstrual symptoms.3

Supplements with some research

CalciumSome 1,200 mg a day for three cycles eased premenstrual symptoms in a trial of 466 women, more than a placebo, though the placebo group also improved and a maker of calcium products helped fund it. Calcium-rich foods count towards the same amount.5
Vitamin B6Early Small trials show mixed results; a review of 9 trials found some benefit, but the trials were of poor quality.6
B6 can damage nerves, sometimes at under 50 mg a day: stop and see a doctor if you feel tingling, burning or numbness. Several products can contain B6, also listed as pyridoxine, so check every label.7
MagnesiumEarly A few small, older trials (28 to 38 women each) reported fewer premenstrual symptoms; another study found no benefit, and reviews call the evidence limited.
Supplements are not tested like medicines and can interact with them. A pharmacist or doctor can check one with you before you start, especially if you take other medicines.
