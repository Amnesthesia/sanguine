What is changing

A typical cycle, drawn to explain. Not your own hormone levels.

Estrogen and progesterone are at their lowest, and bleeding carries iron out of the body. In a study that tracked 397 substances in the blood and urine of 34 women across the cycle, amino acids and vitamin D were higher during the period than in the second half of the cycle.4

Iron, with vitamin C

 Strong

Iron-rich foods such as red meat, chicken, fish, lentils, tofu or leafy greens, with vitamin C from citrus, capsicum or tomato in the same meal.

Monthly bleeding is why women aged 19 to 50 need 18 mg of iron a day, against 8 mg for men.5 Vitamin C in the same meal helps the body absorb iron from plant foods.

Omega-3 from food

 Early

Oily fish such as salmon, sardines or mackerel, or walnuts, chia and linseed.

Why it is only early: the period-pain trials used omega-3 supplements (below). Whether eating it as food eases pain has not been tested. Fish twice a week fits general heart-health advice either way.

Movement that feels good

 Strong

A walk, a swim, yoga or your usual training can lift your mood and energy. Rest is fine too.

Supplements with some research

GingerSome 750 to 2,000 mg of ginger powder a day, in the first 3 to 4 days of the period. A 2024 review of 24 studies found less pain than with a placebo, and no clear difference from anti-inflammatory pain relievers. The trials were small and their results varied.9
Omega-3 (fish oil)Some 300 to 1,800 mg a day for 2 to 3 months. A 2024 review of 12 studies with 881 women found less period pain. Reviews disagree on how large the effect is.9
ZincSome In 6 small trials with 739 women, zinc eased period pain; doses varied widely. More than 40 mg a day over a long time can lower copper levels.10
Supplements are not tested like medicines and can interact with them. A pharmacist or doctor can check one with you before you start, especially if you take other medicines.

How to read the evidence bars

Strong: large reviews or national guidelines agree. Some: several small trials point the same way, but results vary. Early: one study, lab work, or a plausible link that has not been tested in people. General: good advice on any day, not specific to this part of the cycle. Estimates use the same bars for confidence: how sure the app is about a date, from your own data.
